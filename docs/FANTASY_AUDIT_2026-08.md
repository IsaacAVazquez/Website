# Fantasy football roadmap, 2026-08-20

**Method:** five parallel audit passes (model correctness, data pipeline, UX and accessibility, performance, discoverability) over ~25,000 lines across eight routes, each finding then put through an adversarial refutation pass. 37 findings raised, 4 refuted, 33 survived. Three research passes covering the 2026 tool market, free public NFL data sources, and the season calendar, all with live network access. Jest baseline at the time of the audit was green, 59 suites and 570 tests, so none of the fixes below are caught by the current suite.

## The one thing

The two pages whose entire value is the rankings ship HTML that contains no rankings. `src/app/fantasy-football/page.tsx:69` renders the client component with no player data, every player arrives through a `useEffect` fetch in `src/hooks/useFantasySnapshot.ts:174`, and the server response is a header, a tool strip, and three FAQ cards. Meanwhile `public/robots.txt:21-27` explicitly admits OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, and PerplexityBot, none of which run JavaScript. I invited the answer engines in and handed them an empty room, in the three weeks of the year when "2026 WR tiers" is a query anyone actually types.

The second thing shaping everything below is a date rather than a defect. Every board on the surface is a draft board, and the 2026 season opens Wednesday September 9. On that day the rankings become a historical document, the trade calculator starts refusing verdicts by design because its market leg is mock-draft ADP that stops moving, and on October 1 the refresh cron in `.github/workflows/update-fantasy.yml` downshifts to weekly while the freshness bands in `src/lib/fantasyUtils.ts:57` widen from 2 and 4 days to 8 and 14. Three separate pieces of the build assume the season ending is the same event as the drafts ending. So the buckets are peak-draft work now, season-transition work by September 9, and everything else after.

All three research passes had network access, so nothing below is missing because I could not check it. Where a source is unavailable I say so in place.

## What competitors ship that I could match, and with which source

Everything here is free and public, and I have named the exact file or endpoint rather than the vendor's marketing page.

Playoff schedule views, which Establish The Run, Yahoo, Sharp Football, Fantasy Alarm, and League Station all publish in August, come from `https://github.com/nflverse/nfldata/raw/master/data/games.csv`, which `src/lib/nflData.ts` already downloads and `parseCsv` already handles. Opportunity and target share, which is most of what PlayerProfiler sells, comes from `https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/download/stats_player/stats_player_week_2025.csv`, already fetched by `src/lib/fantasyGameLogSource.ts`. Depth chart context, which Draft Sharks bundles, comes from `https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/download/depth_charts/depth_charts_2026.csv`. Cross-draft exposure, which is the entire product for at least four paid competitors, needs no source at all and is a persistence-key change. Rest-of-season and weekly boards, the thing that separates a draft tool from a season tool, come from `https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/rankings/ros-ppr-overall.php` and `https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/rankings/ppr-flex.php`, both free public HTML carrying the same `ecrData` blob the existing scraper parses. Live injury status comes from ESPN's roster endpoint. Waiver trending signal comes from `https://api.sleeper.app/v1/players/nfl/trending/add`, though the join is real work rather than free, since the endpoint returns Sleeper ids and Sleeper's `gsis_id` is null for 8,328 of 12,221 players including Ja'Marr Chase.

One source I looked for and did not find. There is no free current source for 2026 per-team Vegas win totals. Lee Sharpe's `win_totals.csv` is abandoned after 2020 and `initial_lines.csv` after 2021, and ESPN's futures endpoint carries 23 markets for 2026 including division winners and MVP but no per-team over/under. I would rather report that as a gap than substitute something weaker.

ESPN's fantasy API at `lm-api-reads.fantasy.espn.com` deserves its own note, because it is the most tempting thing on the list and the one I am least sure about. One unauthenticated call returned 300 players with ADP, percent owned, auction values, a season projection, and all 18 weekly projections in 1.8 seconds. That would be the first projection source on the site and a third independent ADP reading. It is also undocumented, carries no redistribution grant, and those are ESPN's own projections rather than a consensus, so if it gets built it needs plain attribution, a pinned schema check, and enough separation that it can be pulled without taking anything else with it.

## What I would not build

Sleeper league sync, at least not as the league sync competitors advertise. The CORS header does allow a browser call, so the no-request-time-server-call rule holds and it fits the browser-persisted tools pattern. The ceiling is the problem. I can reprice historical points under a league's custom scoring, but the consensus ranks come from FantasyPros per fixed format and cannot be recomputed, so a custom-scoring board mixes custom history with fixed consensus and the mixture is harder to explain than it is worth.

Sleeper's projections endpoint. The payload is labeled `company: rotowire`, which is a commercial vendor's numbers passed through, and I am not republishing those.

Live scoring during games, and a real-time injury feed treated as a snapshot. Both need request-time external calls, the model doc already says there is no injury feed, and neither fits the pattern the whole surface is built on.

Game-day weather. The free Open-Meteo tier is explicitly non-commercial, the forecast reaches 16 days so Week 1 is out of range today anyway, and `airports.csv` is keyed to home city so it cannot locate the Melbourne game already on the 2026 Week 1 slate. Interesting, not worth it.

An "early sample" badge on the board rows. The caveat that a thin ADP sample carries no market read is already stated in prose in the drawer at `fantasy-football-client.tsx:519-521`, and the model doc deliberately locates it there. Adding a board-level badge on the theory that the caveat is unreachable would be building against a premise that is not true.

Returning `"insufficient"` when both trade packages score zero. Zero is a documented reading rather than a missing one, the model doc says a player at or past a cutoff scores zero, and both players in the example genuinely are past replacement in that league. Relabeling a valid replacement-level answer as an absence would be a regression. The sharper instability is next door, where the relative gap is scale-free at the bottom of the curve and a value of 1.92 against 0 returns a clear edge with supported coverage. If anything gets fixed here, fix that.

Skipping the current season in the game-log builder until Week 18. The rollover is deliberate, documented in the builder comment and twice in the model doc, and the panel always names the season and game count. What is actually wrong is one sentence of copy at `fantasyUtils.ts:371` and one in the doc that both say "prior completed regular season" while the rollover paragraph three paragraphs later says otherwise. Fix the sentences, leave the builder alone.

A mirrored `netlify.toml` header block for `/data/fantasy/**`. The `/data/investments/**` block that already exists does not appear to reach the response either, while the `/_next/static/**` rule does, so adding a second block that does not apply would look like a fix and change nothing. That is the same layer question as the compression finding, which is why I merged them into one investigation rather than two tickets.

Porting the roving tabindex into the two-option draft board toggle, and any of the other places where the honest fix is to make the ARIA match the markup rather than to build the pattern the ARIA promised.

Once the season opened, the [weekly board](/fantasy-football/weekly) became the page that matters. It carries the FantasyPros weekly flex and quarterback consensus with each player's opponent, expert range, and how widely the player is rostered, and it keeps the flex and quarterback ranks separate, because a flex rank of 12 and a quarterback rank of 12 are not the same thing. On September 9 waiver targets moved to their own page at [/fantasy-football/waivers](/fantasy-football/waivers). It lists players whose weekly expert rank runs ahead of how widely they are rostered and prints the rank, the percentile, and the rostered rate beside each gap. It does not model bids or projected points, and it says so.

The weekly board also has a My Team panel now. You can save a roster or import it from the draft tracker, set your league's starting slots, and it builds a weekly lineup from the current ranks and lets you compare a waiver add against a player you would drop. It is stored in your browser like the rest of the fantasy tools.

The draft boards did not go away, but they stopped pretending to be live. The rankings header now reads "Frozen since Sep 10" in a neutral tone instead of warning that the data is aging. I also ran a design review across all eight fantasy pages in the first week of the season, fixed the thirty serious findings it turned up, and re-scored the group from 228 to 274 out of 320. Two refresh failures got fixed along the way. One was FantasyPros listing a single running back on every tight end board, which had been rejecting the whole board since September 2, and the other was the golf refresh failing through off weeks when ESPN posts no field.

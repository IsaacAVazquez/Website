Most of August went into the fantasy tools, since that is when people actually draft. [Best ball](/fantasy-football/best-ball) came first on August 2, with its own snapshot of consensus rankings, Underdog ADP, bye weeks, and Week 17 opponents, a set of dated contest presets, and a [best ball draft tracker](/fantasy-football/best-ball/draft-tracker) that keeps its state apart from the redraft one. A few days later I reworked its recommendations around the roster shapes that tend to advance, which meant scoring completed Week 17 game stacks, waiting until the fifth player from one team before penalizing concentration, and weighing how few players are left in a tier before it falls off. Lobby contests like Weekly Winners and Sit and Go do not get made-up prize economics, since those change from lobby to lobby.

Redraft got lineup settings, so the starting slots you set on the draft tracker now flow through the draft analytics and team value instead of assuming one standard league. Then on August 18 three new pieces shipped together. The [trade calculator](/fantasy-football/trade-calculator) blends expert consensus and mock-draft ADP into a league-specific value, 75/25, and will not call a winner when the board is stale or a player has no reliable market reading. The [mock draft](/fantasy-football/mock-draft) is a practice room on a seeded auto-pick engine. And the [rankings board](/fantasy-football) was rebuilt around tiers, with a separator where the average rank falls off a cliff, a bar on each row showing how much the experts disagree, a sticky control bar, and a player drawer that holds your queue and notes.

I wrote up the thinking behind the [trade calculator](/writing/building-a-fantasy-trade-calculator) and the [best ball room](/writing/building-a-best-ball-draft-room) if you want the longer version.

# Verstappen Swept Singapore While Russell's Engine Penalty Ended His Title Push

Max Verstappen took 33 of the 33 points available to a single driver in Singapore. He won the Saturday sprint, and then [Formula 1's official classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1296/singapore/race-result) has him winning the Grand Prix by 7.335 seconds over Charles Leclerc after 62 laps, which [RacingNews365](https://racingnews365.com/2026-f1-singapore-grand-prix-results) describes as a lights-to-flag win. Kimi Antonelli was third, another seventeen seconds back, and it was Verstappen's second win in a row.

The sprint was where George Russell's weekend went wrong. He took the lead from the front row at the start, then crashed out at the final corner in damp conditions at the end of the first lap, and Verstappen went on to win ahead of Lewis Hamilton and Leclerc, according to [PlanetF1's sprint report](https://www.planetf1.com/news/f1-results-singapore-grand-prix-2026-sprint-race). Antonelli finished fourth for five points, helped by Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri colliding on the last lap while they ran fourth and fifth, which left Norris eighth and Piastri out.

Sunday was worse for Russell in a quieter way. Mercedes fitted additional power unit elements, the [starting grid](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1296/singapore/starting-grid) lists a 60-place penalty, and he started 19th on a street circuit where passing is hard. He finished ninth for two points. After the retirement in Malaysia a week earlier, I think it is fair to read the penalty as the same kind of problem showing up in a different column of the results, and Russell paid for it on the weekend he could least afford it.

The arithmetic is now close to final. Antonelli takes twenty points from the weekend to reach 340, Russell takes two to reach 238, and the lead is 102 with six rounds left and no sprints remaining. Six wins would be worth 150 points, so the title is still technically open, but Russell would need to win nearly everything while Antonelli has problems in several races. Behind them the fight for second is now the more interesting race. Hamilton is on 229, Verstappen on 221, and Leclerc on 215, and Verstappen has scored 58 points in the last two weekends alone.

The pattern I would pull out of Singapore is that the season's second half has belonged to Verstappen on pace and to Antonelli on the table, and Russell has had neither.

# Antonelli Won Austin From Pole and Left Himself Two Points From the Title

Austin was the cleanest win Kimi Antonelli has had in a while, and the timing of it matters. He took pole, kept the lead at the start while Max Verstappen made a poor getaway from second, made one stop from mediums to hards, and won by 13.078 seconds over Charles Leclerc. [Formula 1's official classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1297/united-states/race-result) has Verstappen third and Lewis Hamilton fourth, and it is Antonelli's ninth win of the season. After two weekends that belonged to Verstappen, Antonelli drove away from everyone.

[Autosport's report](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/f1-united-states-gp-antonelli-wins/10868343/) fills in how the rest of the front settled. Leclerc passed Isack Hadjar and Verstappen early with a committed move through the Esses, and Verstappen ran into engine-braking problems later on, called the car undriveable on the radio, and made a second stop that dropped him back before he recovered to third. Leclerc took second from him on lap 49. The only interruptions were two brief Virtual Safety Car periods, one after Oliver Bearman's first-lap contact with Pierre Gasly and one late on after Valtteri Bottas retired with engine trouble.

George Russell's weekend went the way most of his October has gone. He got a five-second penalty for going off the track on the first lap while defending his position, and he finished eighth for four points. Autosport notes that he needed to avoid finishing ninth or lower just to keep his theoretical title chances alive, and he only just did. What that means is that the only open question left is when Antonelli confirms it.

The numbers say that will probably be next weekend. Antonelli moves to 365 points, Russell to 242, and Hamilton to 241, so the lead is 123 with five rounds left. [Formula 1's Mexico City preview](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/its-race-week-5-storylines-were-excited-about-ahead-of-the-2026-mexico-city-grand-prix.3qhXiCGvBjL6deUeE7qpvT) lays out the scenario plainly. A top-nine finish makes him champion, and even tenth or lower is enough unless Russell or Hamilton wins the race. Verstappen on 236 and Leclerc on 233 are close enough to Russell and Hamilton that second place in the championship is a five-way fight, with Lando Norris on 211, across roughly thirty points.

The pattern I would pull out of Austin is that Antonelli did not need anything to go his way this time. Monza and Madrid turned on Virtual Safety Cars, and Austin turned on pole position and a single stop, which is the version of a win that is hardest to argue with.

# Verstappen Won the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, and Russell's Engine Gave Back Baku

The strangest race name of the season belonged to round 16. The Bahrain and Saudi Arabian rounds did not run in the spring because of the instability in the Middle East, and in late July [Speedcafe reported](https://speedcafe.com/f1-news-2026-bahrain-grand-prix-relocation-malaysian-grand-prix-confirmed-return-calendar-update-sepang/) that the Bahrain race would move to Sepang for one year as the Formula 1 Gulf Air Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, filling the gap between Azerbaijan and Singapore. Sepang had been on the calendar for nineteen straight seasons through 2017, and the last driver to win there before it dropped off was Max Verstappen. Nine years later he won there again.

Rain delayed the start by more than an hour, and once it got going Verstappen lost the lead from pole to Kimi Antonelli and George Russell at the start. [News of Bahrain's report](https://www.newsofbahrain.com/en/article/verstappen-wins-dramatic-bahrain-grand-prix-in-malaysia) says he took the lead back after an early safety car and controlled the race from there, and [Formula 1's official classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1308/bahrain/race-result) has him winning by 2.307 seconds over Antonelli after 55 laps. Lewis Hamilton was third after recovering from 18th on an early tire gamble, with Charles Leclerc fourth and Isack Hadjar fifth.

It was Verstappen's first win of 2026, and Red Bull's first with its new Red Bull Ford Powertrains package. I think that matters beyond the trophy. Red Bull went fifteen rounds without a win, and Verstappen's results since Zandvoort, a third at Monza, seconds in Madrid and Baku, and now a win, read like a team that has found real pace.

For the championship, though, the result that mattered was further down the order. Russell was running third with five laps to go when he stopped with what looked like a power unit problem, and he leaves with nothing. Antonelli's eighteen points take him to 320, Russell stays on 236, and the lead is 84 with seven rounds left. The fifteen points Russell won back in Baku a week ago are gone, plus a few more. Hamilton moves to 214, and Leclerc, Verstappen, and Lando Norris are bunched behind him on 191, 188, and 188.

The pattern I would pull out of Sepang is that the driver chasing a big lead cannot afford to be the one losing points to a failure, and with seven races left that is exactly what happened to Russell.

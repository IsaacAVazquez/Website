# Opus 5 Is the Best Model Claude Pro Can Afford to Include

When the Commerce Department lifted its export controls on Claude Fable 5 on June 30, CNBC covered it as [the end of the latest standoff](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-says-trump-admin-has-lifted-export-controls-on-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5.html) between Anthropic and the Trump administration, and when Claude Opus 5 launched on July 24, TechCrunch framed it as [cheaper and less restrictive than Fable 5](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/anthropic-launches-opus-5/). Both are fair readings, and I'd add a third, which is that Fable's new place in the plans and Opus 5 were one decision about what a flat subscription can promise. On July 20 Fable 5 moved to usage credits on the Pro plan and on standard Team seats, and four days later Opus 5 arrived at Opus 4.8's price as what Anthropic calls "the strongest model on Claude Pro." What that means is that July is when Anthropic stopped including its top model in the Pro plan, and I'd argue Opus 5 is as much a packaging decision as it is a research one. I also think Anthropic handled this change far better than it handled the January block on third-party coding tools.

The move to credits is the easier call. Anthropic's June 30 redeployment post [put Fable 5 inside the Pro, Max, Team, and select Enterprise plans](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5) for up to 50% of weekly usage limits through July 7, with usage credits after that, though Anthropic later kept that promotion running until new terms took over on July 20. From July 20 Pro subscribers and standard Team seats pay for Fable 5 with usage credits on top of the subscription, as standard Enterprise seats already had since July 1, while Max and premium seats on Team and seat-based Enterprise plans keep it in the plan for up to half of their weekly usage limits. Anthropic's own explanation in its July 17 announcement was capacity, since demand for Fable had been hard to predict and the company had extended plan access several times as it secured more, and the API price says something similar in dollars, because Fable 5 [lists at $10 and $50 per million input and output tokens](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-fable-5-mythos-5), twice what Anthropic charges for Opus. A list price isn't a disclosed serving cost, so I can't tell whether scarcity or cost binds harder, but I think metering it on Pro is right either way, because a scarce, higher-priced model can't sit inside the cheapest paid plan without rationing everything else in it. My guess is that Max's cap still works because Max buys far more usage, while a capped Fable on Pro would have spent up to half of a much smaller allowance on a model Anthropic says it can't yet supply at scale.

Opus 5 is the other half of that decision, and I think the more interesting one. Anthropic [priced it at $5 and $25 per million tokens](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5), the same as Opus 4.8, and made it the default model on Max. By Anthropic's own benchmark claims, Opus 5 at maximum effort performs within what Anthropic calls 0.5% of Fable 5's peak score on CursorBench 3.2 for half the cost per task, and the company expects the classifiers that screen for cyber misuse to step in about 85% less often than Fable 5's, which matters because Anthropic said on June 30 that Fable's new classifier flagged harmless coding requests more often than the safeguards it replaced. For subscribers that means the best model Pro includes got better on July 24 without the plan having to carry a pricier one. Working on a cross-functional pricing strategy at Civitech left me convinced that deciding what goes in each tier is [product work as much as finance work](/writing/pricing-strategy-initiative), and Opus 5 is a model launch doing that kind of work. It sets the ceiling for what a flat Claude plan includes without a cap, and Fable 5 sits above it, sold by use.

How Anthropic made the change is where July looks best next to January. In January I wrote that Anthropic was [right to keep flat plans out of third-party coding tools and wrong to cut them off overnight](/writing/anthropic-subscription-harness-block-developer-trust), because the block on tools posing as Claude Code came with no notice and spent developer trust on a boundary it could have announced. This time the boundary came with dates, since Fable's place in the plans carried an end date from its June 9 launch, and on the evening of July 17 Anthropic [announced the July 20 terms](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2078302415804379218) three days ahead, with a one-time $100 usage credit for Pro subscribers and standard Team seats. The next morning it [kept Claude Code's weekly limits 50% higher](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2078511173759324328) through August 19 for Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise users, so Pro subscribers lost Fable from the plan but kept the extra Claude Code headroom. Enterprise admins had also gotten [controls on July 2](https://claude.com/blog/giving-admins-more-visibility-and-control-over-claude-usage-and-spend) to set spend limits and choose which models people can use, which a per-model meter needs before a company can trust the bill. I don't want to overstate a $100 credit, which also works as an introduction to paying by use, but a dated change with admin tooling behind it is the opposite of January.

Once the $100 credit is gone, a Pro subscriber who wants Fable 5 pays twice, once for the plan and again in credits, and any rival that puts its best model inside a comparable plan gets an easy pitch to price-sensitive users. My answer depends on Opus 5, because the metered part of Pro is only as large as the gap between Opus 5 and Fable 5, and by Anthropic's own account that gap is small, at least on the coding benchmark it cited. If that holds up in daily use, I think Pro gives up very little, and if it doesn't, Pro users will notice. The other objection, that Fable in the plan was only ever a promotion and so nothing was taken, is true as far as it goes, but calling it a promotion was itself the packaging choice. From Fable's June 9 launch Anthropic gave its place in the plans an end date, reset that date on June 30 after the suspension, and let Pro users work with it inside the plan from July 1 through July 19, and that is a decision about what the plan includes, made in public.

A flat plan should include the best model it can serve without rationing everything else in the plan, and the model above that line should be sold by use, with the terms and dates announced before anything changes. July meets that rule on Pro and standard seats, as far as I can tell, and Max's capped Fable allowance sits in between, as a plan that rations the top model with a cap of half its weekly limits. What would strain it is a widening gap between the model Pro includes and the one it meters, because at some point the credits stop being an extra for heavy users and become the real price of serious work. It would also strain if Opus 5's benchmark results, which are Anthropic's own, don't carry over to the work people actually do with it, since the whole arrangement depends on the ceiling sitting close to the frontier.

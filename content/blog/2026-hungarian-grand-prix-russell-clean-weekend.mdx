# Norris Won in Hungary and Russell Lost the Ground He Needed

A week after Spa, the Hungarian Grand Prix was the race where I expected George Russell to start taking points back from Kimi Antonelli, and it went close to the opposite way. Lando Norris put his McLaren on pole and [won by a little over fifteen seconds from Max Verstappen](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1291/hungary/race-result), Antonelli climbed from seventh on the grid to third, and Russell finished seventh, nearly a minute behind the winner. It was McLaren's first win of the season, and it came on an afternoon when the two Mercedes drivers finished third and seventh.

Qualifying set most of it up. Norris took pole by twelve thousandths of a second from Lewis Hamilton, but Hamilton picked up a three-place grid penalty for impeding another driver and started fifth, and Antonelli, who qualified fourth, took the same three-place drop for failing to slow for yellow flags and started seventh. That left Charles Leclerc alongside Norris on the front row, with Oscar Piastri and Verstappen behind them, and on a circuit where passing is hard, a clean run from pole is most of the race. Norris got it, and a fifteen-second winning margin at the Hungaroring says nobody behind him had an answer.

For the championship, what I would pull out of the result is how well Antonelli limited the damage from a bad Saturday. He started seventh and finished third, behind a Verstappen whose second place matched his result in Austria as his best of the year. Russell started one place ahead of Antonelli, sixth, and went backwards to seventh, which is the part of the afternoon he cannot afford. Hamilton came home fifth with a five-second penalty for speeding in the pit lane on top of his grid drop, Leclerc was fourth and set the fastest lap, and Piastri retired fifteen laps from the end.

The standings moved in Antonelli's favor anyway. He goes into the summer break on 219 points, with Hamilton second on 169 and Russell third on 160, so his lead over second place grew from 45 to 50 and his gap to Russell went from 50 to 59. After [losing everything on the opening lap at Spa](/writing/2026-belgian-grand-prix-antonelli-title-lead), Russell needed a weekend that took a real bite out of that number, and instead he added six points to Antonelli's fifteen. I think that matters more than who won, because Russell's case for this title has always been that he is right there whenever his car holds together, and in Hungary he finished the race and still came home seventh.

Norris's win is worth a paragraph of its own. Back in [Miami](/writing/2026-miami-grand-prix-mclaren-mercedes) I wrote that McLaren would win races this year, probably soon, and it took until the eleventh race of the season for one to arrive, since every race before this one went to Mercedes or Ferrari. It moves Norris to 128 points and fifth in the standings, and with Piastri retiring it was a one-car result, and one race is not enough to say McLaren have caught Mercedes across a full weekend. I would read it as McLaren being quick enough at the right track, which is still a real step from China, where neither car started.

The pattern I would pull out of Hungary is that this title race does not only turn on Mercedes reliability. Antonelli lost three grid places on Saturday and still gained points on both of his nearest rivals, and Russell had a weekend with nothing broken that I can see on the result sheet and lost nine more points to his teammate. The season stops now until Zandvoort on August 23, and Antonelli carries a fifty-point lead into the break.

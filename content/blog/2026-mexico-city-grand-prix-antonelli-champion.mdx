# Antonelli Clinched the Title in Mexico City on a Day Verstappen Won the Race

Kimi Antonelli is the 2026 world champion. He finished third in the Mexico City Grand Prix behind Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton, and [PlanetF1's race report](https://www.planetf1.com/news/mexican-grand-prix-2026-race-report) puts it simply, calling him the new and youngest world champion in Formula 1 history. He is 20, and [RacingNews365](https://racingnews365.com/kimi-antonelli-crowned-ferrari-implode-as-mexico-delivers-title-chaos-winners-losers-from-the-mexico-city-gp) adds that he is the first Italian to win the drivers' title since Alberto Ascari in 1953. He leads Verstappen by 119 points with four rounds left and only 100 points still available to anyone, so the title is settled whatever happens in Brazil, Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi.

The race itself belonged to Verstappen, and it took a while to get there. Hamilton put the Ferrari on pole, Verstappen started fifth after a one-place grid penalty for a practice collision with Liam Lawson, and Charles Leclerc led early after he and Hamilton tangled on the opening lap, which Hamilton described on the radio as getting pushed out by his own teammate. Leclerc then picked up a five-second penalty for leaving the track and gaining an advantage. When he pitted at the end of lap 27 to serve it, Verstappen came in right behind him and took the net lead, and he held it for the rest of the afternoon.

It was a long and interrupted afternoon. Both Aston Martins retired in the first handful of laps, with Lance Stroll's stop bringing out a Virtual Safety Car, and later Valtteri Bottas crashed heavily at Turn 1 after a front suspension failure on his Cadillac, which brought out the safety car and then a red flag for barrier repairs. Bottas walked away from it. The race restarted from a standing start on lap 47, and a second safety car came out on lap 59 after Arvid Lindblad hit the wall at Turn 2 following contact with Oscar Piastri, who took a ten-second penalty for it. [Formula 1's official classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1298/mexico/race-result) has Verstappen winning by 0.799 seconds over Hamilton, with Antonelli another 3.2 seconds back, Leclerc fourth, Lando Norris fifth, and George Russell sixth after a ten-second penalty for a collision with Isack Hadjar.

What I think is most worth taking from the last two months is how the title was actually won. After Baku in late September the lead was 66 and Russell had just won. In the four weekends since, Malaysia, Singapore, Austin, and Mexico City, Antonelli scored 78 points and Russell scored 14, between a power unit retirement while running third, a sprint crash, a 60-place engine penalty, and two penalties for first-lap or racing incidents. Verstappen scored 98 over the same four weekends and has moved up to second in the standings on 261, ahead of Hamilton on 259 and Russell on 250. Antonelli won the most races this season, nine of them, but the championship was decided in October by the driver chasing him running out of clean weekends.

The pattern I would pull out of the season is that Antonelli built his lead early enough that the October weekends where Verstappen was faster only decided who finishes second.

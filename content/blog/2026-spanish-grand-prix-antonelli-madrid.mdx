# Madrid Went to Antonelli on a Virtual Safety Car Norris Could Not Use

The Spanish Grand Prix moved to Madrid this year, and the race on the Madring came down to one pit entry. Lando Norris took pole by eleven thousandths of a second over Kimi Antonelli and was leading when Lance Stroll's Aston Martin stopped on track and brought out a Virtual Safety Car. [RacingNews365's report](https://racingnews365.com/2026-f1-spanish-grand-prix-madrid-results) says Norris just missed the pit entry, Antonelli pitted, and he came back out ahead of both Norris and Max Verstappen, which it fairly describes as a straightforward cruise to his eighth win of the season from there.

[Formula 1's official classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1294/spain/race-result) has Antonelli winning by 4.351 seconds over Verstappen, with Norris third, another 0.7 seconds back. I would argue Norris was the fastest driver in Madrid across the weekend and leaves with fifteen points, while the driver who started next to him on the front row leaves with twenty-five. A VSC during a stop costs a fraction of what a normal stop costs, so the cars that get in under it gain a large chunk of time on anyone who misses it, and Norris missed it by a few meters.

The championship side of the weekend was worse for everyone chasing Antonelli. George Russell finished fifth, a long way back behind Charles Leclerc, and Lewis Hamilton retired on lap six with a brake failure, his first retirement of the season. Antonelli moves to 292 points, Russell to 211, and the lead is 81 with nine rounds left. Hamilton stays on 191, and Norris climbs to 186, which puts three drivers within about twenty-five points of each other in the fight for second while the fight for first keeps getting further away.

What I think is worth paying attention to is how Antonelli is collecting these wins. Monza came from 19th on the grid with a VSC at the right time, and Madrid came from second on the grid with a VSC at the right time. He still had to pass Russell on track at Monza and be close enough to Norris to use the break in Madrid, but both wins turned on timing he did not control. At some point the luck evens out, and the question for Russell is whether there are enough races left for that to matter.

The pattern I would pull out of Madrid is that an 81-point lead is now big enough that Antonelli can lose a race outright to a problem and still lead comfortably.

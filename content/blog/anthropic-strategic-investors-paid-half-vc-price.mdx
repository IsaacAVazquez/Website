# Anthropic's Cloud Partners Bought In at Less Than Half What VCs Reportedly Offered

There were two prices for a piece of Anthropic in April, and which one applied depended on what the buyer brought with them. By April 15, Bloomberg had reported venture offers to fund the company at about $800 billion or more, and [TechCrunch reported that day that Anthropic hadn't accepted them](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/15/anthropic-shrugs-off-vc-funding-offers-valuing-it-at-800b-for-now/). Within ten days, Amazon and Google agreed to put in a combined $15 billion at roughly the price of Anthropic's February round, less than half what the venture investors were reportedly offering. The reports noted both numbers but treated the $800 billion offers as background to the compute deals and hung the circular financing worry over the Amazon deal, without asking what the gap meant. I think that gap is the most informative number of the month, because it says compute is the input Anthropic is shortest on, and that shortage lets the companies that own chips and power set the terms.

## The price Amazon and Google paid

The baseline is February 12, when Anthropic [raised $30 billion at a $380 billion post-money valuation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-30-billion-series-g-funding-380-billion-post-money-valuation), which works out to about $350 billion before the new money. Amazon's April 20 deal is $5 billion now and up to $20 billion more tied to commercial milestones, and [GeekWire reported](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/amazon-doubles-down-on-anthropic-with-25b-investment-mirroring-its-openai-cloud-deal/) that the first $5 billion values Anthropic at $380 billion. Google's deal, reported April 24, is [$10 billion now at a $350 billion valuation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/google-to-invest-up-to-40b-in-anthropic-in-cash-and-compute/) plus up to $30 billion more if Anthropic hits performance targets, terms TechCrunch attributed to Anthropic. Those match the post-money and pre-money values of the February round, so I read both deals as priced at February. The venture number kept climbing, and by April 29 TechCrunch's sources were describing [offers to raise about $50 billion at $850 billion to $900 billion](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/sources-anthropic-could-raise-a-new-50b-round-at-a-valuation-of-900b/). At $800 billion the same $15 billion buys less than half the stake, so on the headline figures Amazon and Google got roughly twice the ownership per dollar that the venture offers implied, though none of the reports say whether the venture numbers are pre-money or post-money.

## What the discount bought

Why would a company fielding offers at $800 billion sell shares at February's price? The answer is what came with the money. Anthropic committed [more than $100 billion over ten years to AWS](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute) for up to 5 gigawatts of new capacity, with meaningful capacity promised within three months and nearly a gigawatt of Trainium2 and Trainium3 by the end of 2026. The Google deal brings 5 gigawatts of Google Cloud compute over five years, next to an April 6 agreement with Google and Broadcom for multiple gigawatts of TPUs starting in 2027. CoreWeave signed a multi-year deal on April 10, with capacity arriving in phases from later in 2026. Apart from the AWS promises for 2026 and CoreWeave's first phases, the capacity with dates attached mostly arrives in 2027 or later, and Google Cloud's five-year schedule has no disclosed start, so I read the equity discount as part of what Anthropic paid to lock in dated capacity.

## Why compute is the scarce input

The evidence that compute is the binding constraint is mostly Anthropic's own. On April 6 it said [run-rate revenue had passed $30 billion](https://www.anthropic.com/news/google-broadcom-partnership-compute), up from about $9 billion at the end of 2025, and that business customers on an annualized pace of more than $1 million had doubled to more than 1,000 in under two months. Run-rate annualizes a recent stretch, so on a curve this steep it runs ahead of trailing revenue, but it tracks the current rate of paid demand, which is closer to what capacity has to serve. From April 4, subscription limits stopped covering third-party harnesses such as OpenClaw, moving that use to paid extra usage and extending the line I wrote about in [my January piece on the subscription block](/writing/anthropic-subscription-harness-block-developer-trust). Anthropic's April 20 post said consumer growth had hurt reliability for free and paid users, especially at peak hours. On April 21 the pricing page began showing Claude Code as excluded from the $20 Pro plan, which head of growth Amol Avasare called [a test on about 2 percent of new prosumer signups](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/22/anthropic_removes_claude_code_pro/). The plan changes could also be margin protection on flat-rate plans that heavy agent use made expensive, and I think that's part of it, but peak-hour reliability is a capacity problem, and Anthropic named it in the post where it signed for up to 5 gigawatts. That's the customer's side of the signal in [my April 28 piece on Q1 earnings](/writing/reading-q1-2026-earnings-ai-capex-lens), where constrained capacity plus rising spend means real demand.

## The case that the gap is smaller than it looks

The strongest objection is that only the $15 billion up front is priced at February, and the contingent money that lifts the headlines to $25 billion from Amazon and $40 billion from Google has no disclosed price, so if those tranches are priced near the market when they're drawn, the discount covers less than a quarter of the headline totals. I'd guess both deals were priced off February before the venture offers surfaced, and preemptive offers nobody has closed make a weak benchmark. I accept most of that, and the real gap is probably smaller than the headline. A bundle reading runs the other way. The more than $100 billion committed to AWS is more than four times the $25 billion Amazon could invest, and GeekWire set the deal beside Amazon's $50 billion OpenAI investment two months earlier, so the discount could be a rebate for landing a $100 billion customer while Amazon also courts the other top lab. Because the equity was priced with the compute contract, the share price alone can't show who set the terms. I still land on the suppliers holding the bargaining power, because what Anthropic needed was capacity with dates attached, and on April's schedules that capacity was scarce enough that shopping around wouldn't have bought much more of it. In October 2025 I argued that [circularity amplifies whatever the end demand turns out to be](/writing/ai-circular-financing-2025), and that the first way it turns dangerous is when the loop stands in for that demand. Anthropic's run-rate and its more than 1,000 business customers spending over $1 million a year say there's paying demand at the end of this one.

April's deals say that the companies that own chips and power could put $15 billion into a lab whose run-rate had more than tripled since the end of 2025, at under half the headline venture price, and I think the main reason is that they were selling capacity on a schedule, which cash alone can't buy. I think Anthropic was right to take those terms, since demand it can't serve costs more than the dilution does, but the terms show who had more bargaining power in April. What would shift that power back is capacity catching up with demand, which on April's schedules mostly arrives from 2027 on, when the TPU gigawatts come online and a buyer spread across several suppliers can play them against each other. The next test is when a cloud partner next buys Anthropic stock, since a price close to whatever venture investors are paying would suggest compute has stopped being the thing Anthropic is shortest on.

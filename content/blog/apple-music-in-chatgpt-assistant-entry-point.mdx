# Apple Music Inside ChatGPT Is Good for Apple Music and a Warning for Siri

Going by the headlines, December's biggest Apple AI story was John Giannandrea. [Apple said on December 1](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/12/john-giannandrea-to-retire-from-apple/) that he is stepping down as senior vice president for Machine Learning and AI Strategy and will advise the company until he retires in spring 2026, with Amar Subramanya joining as vice president of AI reporting to Craig Federighi. The first round of coverage read it as fallout from the Siri delays, with MacRumors tying the retirement to Siri in its headline and The Register framing it as Apple swapping one former Google AI chief for another. When OpenAI announced on December 16 that Apple Music would be an app inside ChatGPT, the coverage I saw treated it as product news. My read is that the Apple Music story says more about where Apple stands. Having Apple Music inside ChatGPT is good for a subscription that has to be wherever listeners are, and it's also a warning for Siri, because every Apple service that works inside someone else's assistant adds one more task people can start somewhere other than Siri.

## Apple's own chart

The clearest public signal came from Apple itself. On December 10 Apple published its lists of the most downloaded apps of 2025, and [ChatGPT was the top free iPhone app in the US](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/10/apple-most-downloaded-apps-2025/), ahead of Threads, Google, TikTok, and WhatsApp, with Google's Gemini at tenth. On Apple's 2024 list ChatGPT was fourth. Every iPhone comes with Siri, and in 2025 the free app with the most US downloads was a different assistant, with a second one in the top ten.

The fair objection is that a download chart counts downloads, and a download isn't use. The list doesn't say how often anyone opens ChatGPT, what they ask it, or whether any of it is something they used to ask Siri, and I haven't seen a public number that measures where people start tasks on their phones. So I'd read the chart narrowly, as Apple's own storefront telling Apple which assistant people actually went out and got when they already had one built in, which is a smaller claim than ChatGPT replacing Siri.

## Apple Music in ChatGPT's directory

On December 16, Fidji Simo, OpenAI's CEO of Applications, [announced that Apple Music would be one of the apps](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/16/apple-music-is-coming-to-chatgpt-openai-announces/) in a new directory inside ChatGPT, alongside Adobe, Airtable, OpenTable, Replit, Salesforce, and others. [It went live the next day](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/17/chatgpt-apple-music-integration/), letting people search Apple Music's catalog and build playlists by asking for them once they connect an Apple Account. None of the coverage I read included a comment from Apple or said whether Apple or OpenAI built the app. Either way, I think ChatGPT is the right place for Apple Music to be. [Spotify has been available inside ChatGPT since October 6](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/06/openai-launches-apps-inside-of-chatgpt/), so a request for a party playlist could already bring up the Spotify app, and a music service that isn't there when that request comes in misses it. I argued in October that being inside ChatGPT is becoming [a channel apps can't afford to skip, and that the channel gets to set the terms](/writing/openai-devday-2025-platform-play). Apple Music fits the first half, and the second half is the unusual part for Apple, which is used to being the company that sets the terms.

The Siri side is where I'd worry. When Apple built ChatGPT into Siri with [iOS 18.2 in December 2024](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/12/apple-intelligence-now-features-image-playground-genmoji-and-more/), it kept Siri in front. Siri can suggest sending certain requests to ChatGPT and then shows ChatGPT's answer directly, and by default nobody needs a ChatGPT account to use it. Apple Music inside ChatGPT runs the other way, with ChatGPT in front and an Apple service behind it. The strongest counterargument is that this is one more distribution channel for Apple Music, and that Apple gets paid when ChatGPT grows, since it takes its commission when someone subscribes to ChatGPT through the App Store. I'd grant both. But a commission pays Apple for ChatGPT's growth and does nothing about the habits that come with it. Someone who gets used to asking ChatGPT for music has one less reason to hold the side button for Siri, and I'd argue where people start is worth more to Apple over time than the commission is.

## Japan and the side button

Japan is where the entry point itself is opening up, because Apple is changing iOS there to comply with a new law. On December 17, a day before Japan's Mobile Software Competition Act took full effect, [Apple announced its changes for Japan](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/12/apple-announces-changes-to-ios-in-japan/), including alternative app marketplaces people can set as their default, alternative in-app payments and links to buy on the web, and browser and search engine choice screens. The changes also let people in Japan set the iPhone's side button, the one that calls up Siri, to launch a third-party voice assistant instead, an option Apple had documented for developers in November. The side button is the part I'd watch. It's the iPhone's hardware shortcut to an assistant, and I think a default like that does a lot of the work of keeping tasks starting in Siri. In Japan it's a setting a rival assistant can compete for. It's one country, and people have to go change the setting themselves, so I wouldn't expect many of them to switch right away. But Apple can no longer count on the default alone to keep tasks starting in Siri there, and with a rival assistant at the top of Apple's own US download chart, I don't think it should assume the default is holding anywhere else either.

If I had to pick one thing to track in 2026, it would be where people start, meaning whether the first thing someone reaches for on an iPhone is Siri or another company's assistant. In September I argued that [Apple was executing well on hardware while the value moved to the model layer](/writing/apple-iphone-17-ai-strategy-2025), where it's behind, and that the delayed Siri was the real subplot. [Bloomberg has reported](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/05/google-gemini-1-billion-deal-apple-siri/) that the rebuilt Siri is on track for spring 2026, and that Apple was finalizing a deal to pay Google roughly $1 billion a year for a 1.2 trillion parameter Gemini model to handle Siri's planning and summarizing, which neither company has confirmed. Whatever model it runs on, the spring Siri has to handle the everyday requests ChatGPT's apps are built to take, like that party playlist, and finish them faster from the side button than ChatGPT can from its own app. It also has to deliver the personal context and app integration Apple promised and then delayed, which I think is the part of the job a built-in assistant is best placed to do. A Siri that does that makes Apple Music in ChatGPT look like sensible distribution for a service that should be everywhere, and a Siri that doesn't leaves Apple collecting its commission while more of what people do on an iPhone starts in someone else's app, which is a weaker position than the one Apple holds at the end of 2025, even if the revenue doesn't show it for a while.

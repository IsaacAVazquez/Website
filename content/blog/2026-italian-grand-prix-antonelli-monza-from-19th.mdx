# Antonelli Won Monza From 19th, and Russell Lost the One Race He Had to Win

If there was ever a weekend set up for George Russell to take a real bite out of the championship, it was this one. Kimi Antonelli took a new power unit before Monza and started the Italian Grand Prix 19th, Pierre Gasly put the Alpine on pole, and Russell started near the front with the faster car. Antonelli won anyway. [Formula 1's official classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1293/italy/race-result) reads Antonelli, Russell, Max Verstappen, with the gap between the two Mercedes cars at 3.857 seconds after 53 laps, and the race that was supposed to close the title fight ended up widening it.

The context coming in matters. Zandvoort two weeks earlier went to Lando Norris, his second win in a row, with Antonelli second and Russell third, which left Antonelli on 242 points and Russell and Lewis Hamilton level on 183. Russell needed the races where Antonelli started out of position, and Monza was the clearest one he was likely to get.

For a while it looked like he would take it. Russell passed Gasly for the lead at the start of the second lap, and then Charles Leclerc crashed out in front of the Ferrari crowd on the opening laps and brought out the red flag while barrier repairs were made. At the stoppage Antonelli was still back in twelfth. What turned the race was the Virtual Safety Car when Lance Stroll's Aston Martin stopped with a hydraulics problem around lap 29. [The FIA's race report](https://api.fia.com/news/f1-antonelli-storms-19th-grid-take-sensational-home-win-monza-ahead-russell-and-verstappen) says Antonelli and Verstappen took fresh tires under the VSC while Russell stayed out and inherited the lead on increasingly worn hards. From there it was a long chase. Antonelli caught him at the end of lap 48, ran wide on his first attempt, and made the pass stick a lap later, with three laps to go.

I think you can defend Mercedes' call on Russell's strategy, since staying out under a VSC to keep track position is a reasonable gamble and it nearly worked. But what that means for the championship is harsh. Russell drove a strong race, finished second, and lost seven points to a teammate who started 19th. Antonelli moves to 267 with his seventh win of the season, Russell to 201, and the lead is 66, with Hamilton third on 191 after a quiet sixth place. Norris was fourth on the day and is fourth in the table on 171.

There is also the part of this that has nothing to do with points. Antonelli is the first Italian to win the Italian Grand Prix since Ludovico Scarfiotti in 1966, and he did it from the back of the grid in a Mercedes, which is about as unlikely a way to end that wait as anyone could have written.

The pattern I would pull out of Monza is that Russell's path to the title now depends on races going wrong for Antonelli in ways that a grid penalty no longer covers. A 19th-place start was supposed to be the handicap, and it cost Antonelli nothing.

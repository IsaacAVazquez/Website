# Anthropic's Enterprise Lead Is Real and Narrower Than the 40 Percent Headline

The number I kept seeing next to Anthropic's name in December was 40 percent. [Menlo Ventures published it](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/) on December 9 as Anthropic's share of enterprise LLM spend, the [joint release announcing the Accenture Anthropic Business Group](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-accenture-partnership) repeated it the same day as Anthropic's enterprise market share, and it arrived in a month when coverage was already reading Anthropic as the enterprise company with momentum. I think the lead is real, and I also think the 40 percent answers a narrower question than the one it's being used to answer. It's a share of API spend, estimated from a survey by one of Anthropic's investors, and Menlo attributes the company's rise to coding, while a share of paying businesses puts it well behind OpenAI. I read the Accenture and Snowflake deals as attempts to turn a deep lead with developers into a broad one, and whether that works is the real test of the enterprise story.

## What the 40 percent measures

Menlo's figure estimates API spend, meaning what companies pay to call a model from their own software. The report bases its model shares on each provider's proportion of production API usage, from a survey of 495 US enterprise AI decision-makers run from November 7 to 25, and it lists Anthropic as a Menlo portfolio company. In Menlo's series, Anthropic went from 12 percent in 2023 to 24 percent in 2024 to 40 percent in 2025, while OpenAI fell from 50 percent to 27 percent and Google rose from 7 percent to 21 percent. Menlo discloses the investor tie and ran the survey with an independent research firm, so I don't think it sinks the number. I take the trend as real, as a measure of where API money goes.

## The lead is deepest in coding

In the same model-share estimate, Menlo puts Anthropic at 54 percent of coding, up from 42 percent in its mid-year update six months earlier, against 21 percent for OpenAI, and credits much of the gain to Claude Code. The product's own number supports that. When Anthropic [announced the acquisition of Bun](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-acquires-bun-as-claude-code-reaches-usd1b-milestone), the JavaScript runtime Claude Code ships on, it said Claude Code reached a $1 billion annualized run-rate in November, six months after it became publicly available. Where I'd push back is the restatement. The Accenture release footnotes Menlo for its line that Claude Code "now holds over half of the AI coding market," which moves a model provider's estimated share of API usage onto a single product. Menlo sizes the market for coding products in a different section, at $4.0 billion of departmental AI spending in 2025. Over half of that would be more than $2 billion spent during the year, and Claude Code only reached a $1 billion annualized run-rate in November, so its revenue for 2025 came in well short of that. My read is that coding is the strongest fact in Anthropic's enterprise story, and the release claimed more for one product than the report measured.

## Ramp's share of paying businesses

In a [December 8 story](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/08/openai-boasts-enterprise-win-days-after-internal-code-red-on-google-threat/), TechCrunch cited the Ramp AI Index for close to 36 percent of US businesses paying OpenAI, a figure the story labeled as ChatGPT Enterprise customers, against 14.3 percent for Anthropic. That's a share of businesses paying a provider, and it comes from a different population than Menlo's, since Menlo surveyed 495 enterprise decision-makers and Ramp counts the businesses on its own platform. So Anthropic can take the largest share of API dollars while, in the figures TechCrunch cited, about two and a half times as many businesses pay OpenAI as pay Anthropic. Someone defending the headline would say the dollars are what matter. On November 16, [60 Minutes reported](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-warning-of-ai-potential-dangers-60-minutes-transcript/) that 80 percent of Anthropic's revenue comes from businesses, and a share of businesses weighs a ten-seat subscription the same as a nine-figure contract. I'd grant that spend is the right measure of where Anthropic stood in December 2025. Breadth is the better measure of how durable that position is, because a lead whose growth Menlo credits mainly to one workload is exposed to one competitor getting better at that workload. Together they say OpenAI is ahead on how many businesses pay it while Anthropic is ahead on how much companies spend through its API.

## Accenture, Snowflake, and Slack

Durability is the test I'd apply to December's deals, and they differ in how far past developers they reach. Accenture will train about 30,000 of its professionals on Claude and give Claude Code to tens of thousands of its developers. The part that reaches past developers is the industry offerings the two are developing, starting with financial services, life sciences, healthcare, and the public sector. The [Snowflake agreement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/snowflake-anthropic-expanded-partnership) announced December 3 is a multi-year, $200 million deal that makes Claude models available to more than 12,600 Snowflake customers, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 powers Snowflake Intelligence, an agent that lets people get answers from company data by asking in plain language. Anthropic's announcement doesn't say who pays whom or what the $200 million covers, so I read the deal as distribution and the 12,600 as reach. [Claude Code in Slack](https://claude.com/blog/claude-code-and-slack), in beta as a research preview since December 8, is the one still pointed mostly at engineers. Tagging @Claude on a coding request starts a Claude Code session with the thread as context, so a task can start where the problem was reported.

Coding is the clearest case of the [scoped, checkable work I wrote about in September](/writing/agentic-ai-production-reality-check-fall-2025), since a test suite tells you whether the output works. That's part of why I don't assume the coding lead carries over on its own into the regulated industries the partnership named, where output is harder to check. My October argument, that [Agent Skills was the right platform bet](/writing/anthropic-agent-skills-written-procedures-platform-bet) because it moves customization from wiring up tools to writing down procedures, matters here too, since procedures are something a compliance team can actually write. I think the industry offerings and the Snowflake distribution are the right moves for that, and the way to judge them is whether Claude usage grows among people who don't write code.

Put together, December's numbers describe a lead that runs deep with developers and trails on breadth, and the number that would change my read is the business share. If Anthropic's share of paying businesses climbs and Menlo's next report shows it gaining in categories other than coding, the lead is broadening, and the Accenture and Snowflake channels are likely part of why. If the 40 percent keeps rising while Ramp's business share stays near 14 percent, that fits more than one story. Anthropic could be selling more to the customers it already has, or it could be winning large enterprises that Ramp's panel doesn't see well, and a share of spend can also rise when rivals' spend shrinks. Only Anthropic's own customer numbers, reported over time and by size, would tell those apart, and until then I'd read the 40 percent as a measure of how much companies spend with Anthropic and the business share as the measure of how many.

I built [Before You Buy](/investments/before-you-buy) because I wanted a stock research page to answer a question about the whole portfolio. If I add this company, how does the mix change? A company can be interesting on its own, but I still want to see how much of the portfolio it would occupy and how its price history relates to what I already hold.

The tool starts with holdings saved in the [Investment Research Platform](/investments), a labeled $50,000 sample, or a portfolio I edit directly on the page. I enter a stock and an amount, then compare that purchase with the same amount in another stock or fund. Both choices add new money to the same starting portfolio. Edits stay in a separate browser draft, so trying a different mix leaves the holdings saved on Investments intact.

I put the resulting position size first, followed by a sentence explaining how direct sector exposure changes under each option. The comparison puts the current portfolio beside both proposed mixes, with the largest holding, historical sensitivity to the S&P 500, and average returns on the benchmark's five worst days. The sector bars use the same colors and ordering across all three mixes. I think this makes the tradeoff easier to see than asking someone to interpret a paragraph of statistics.

I framed this as an independent product concept for Google Finance. The useful interaction is the comparison itself, because it gives the research a specific decision to support. The calculations use committed closing-price snapshots and the same historical dates for every holding, both purchase choices, and SPY, the market benchmark. A daily return is included only when all of those symbols have prices on both consecutive benchmark trading dates. I withhold the historical comparisons when fewer than 30 daily returns are available.

Both portfolio mixes keep their weights fixed across that window. Beta measures historical sensitivity to the benchmark, correlation compares each purchase with the existing mix, and the stress view uses the benchmark's five lowest daily returns. Funds remain a separate allocation because the data does not contain their underlying holdings, so the direct sector totals leave that exposure unassigned. The comparison excludes dividends, fees, and taxes, and I treat the results as descriptions of past behavior, with the purchase decision left to the reader.

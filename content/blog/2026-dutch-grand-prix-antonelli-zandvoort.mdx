# Norris Won Zandvoort's Farewell and the Title Gap Did Not Move

The summer break was supposed to be the month George Russell used to reset, and the Dutch Grand Prix gave him a good weekend and nothing to show for it in the standings. Lando Norris won from pole for the second race in a row, [11.536 seconds clear of Kimi Antonelli with Russell third](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1292/netherlands/race-result), and Max Verstappen's home race ended before he had completed a lap. Russell won the sprint on Saturday and stood on the podium on Sunday, and Antonelli still left the Netherlands exactly as far ahead of him as he arrived.

This was the last Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort for the foreseeable future, with the race falling off the calendar after this season, and the whole event was built around Verstappen. It did not go his way at any point. He qualified seventh and then retired on the opening lap, so the crowd that came for a send-off watched the rest of the afternoon without him. Norris, meanwhile, took pole by about a tenth from Russell and turned it into a win by more than eleven seconds, which makes it two in a row for McLaren after [Hungary](/writing/2026-hungarian-grand-prix-russell-clean-weekend).

For the championship, the arithmetic is straightforward and a little cruel. Antonelli scored 23 points across the weekend, five for fourth in the sprint and eighteen for second on Sunday, and Russell scored exactly the same 23, eight for the sprint win and fifteen for third. So Antonelli moves to 242 and Russell to 183, the same 59-point gap they had after Hungary. Lewis Hamilton finished fourth and is also on 183, level with Russell for second. What that means is that a sprint win and a Grand Prix podium bought Russell nothing against the driver he is chasing, because Antonelli took fourth in the sprint and then finished in front of him on Sunday.

The reason I would still resist calling 59 points decisive is the same one I have been making all year. Antonelli has been the fastest driver in the field for most of the season, and the races where he lost big points were the ones where the car let him down, in Barcelona and at Silverstone, while Russell's biggest losses came from the failure in Canada and the opening-lap retirement at Spa. None of those announced themselves in advance, and one landing on Antonelli in September would change the arithmetic in an afternoon. Russell's job has not changed since July. He has to win the weekends where nothing goes wrong and be close enough to collect when something does, and the difficulty at Zandvoort is that he did his part and it was Norris who collected.

Ferrari left Zandvoort with the quieter story. Hamilton and Charles Leclerc finished fourth and fifth, Leclerc set the fastest lap, and the team that won two races in the early summer has not won since Silverstone. Norris is the more interesting name behind the Mercedes pair now. He scored 31 points across the weekend, more than anyone else, and he is fourth in the standings on 159, which is still a long way back, but I think it is the first stretch this year where McLaren have looked like the team taking points off everyone, Mercedes included.

The pattern I would pull out of Zandvoort is that the break changed less than the result sheet suggests. A McLaren won for the second straight race, but the gap at the top of the championship is exactly where it was in July, and with Russell and Hamilton tied on 183, Antonelli now has two drivers 59 points behind him instead of one. I built [Formula 1 Pulse](/formula-1) to keep that ledger as the season runs toward its end, and after this weekend it says what it has said since spring, only with fewer races left to argue about it.

# Microsoft Is Selling Enterprises the Layer Around the Model, and That's the Right Bet

Two stories ran through the coverage of Microsoft's Ignite week. The first treated the Anthropic partnership, announced on November 18, as Microsoft hedging against OpenAI, and D.A. Davidson analyst Gil Luria told Reuters, in a quote [Ars Technica carried](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2025/11/tech-giants-pour-billions-into-anthropic-as-circular-ai-investments-roll-on/), that Microsoft had chosen not to rely on a single frontier model company. Ars itself framed the deal as another round of circular AI investment. The second was that Copilot has a demand problem, which CNBC reported on November 23 through [some IT buyers who want to cut their Copilot seats to zero](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/23/microsoft-faces-uphill-climb-to-win-in-ai-chatbots-with-copilot.html). Both describe something real, but I read the week as one decision to sell enterprises the governed layer around the models, meaning the identities, permissions, and registry every agent needs, whoever built the agent and whichever lab trained its model. I think that's the right bet, even though it probably means the assistant people ask for by name will belong to one of the labs.

## Agent 365 manages agents like employees

CNBC described Agent 365 as [a control plane with a registry, access controls, dashboards, interoperability, and security](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/18/microsoft-unveils-agent-365-to-help-companies-control-track-ai-agents.html) for agents built on Microsoft's platforms or on others like Adobe, ServiceNow, and Workday. Judson Althoff, the CEO of Microsoft's commercial business, pitched it as giving agents identity and access the way companies provision employees. It's in Microsoft's Frontier early-access program, with no price announced. Microsoft's Ignite Book of News says Agent 365 extends the infrastructure companies use to manage users, including Entra, Microsoft's identity service, to agents. I argued in September that the teams getting value from agents [manage them the way they'd manage a capable new hire](/writing/agentic-ai-production-reality-check-fall-2025), with bounded responsibility and access to only what they need, and Agent 365 turns that idea into an admin console. My verdict is that this is where enterprise switching costs will sit. Once a company's agents have identities and permissions in one registry, changing vendors starts to look like moving every employee account to a new system, and since the registry takes agents from other platforms, none of that should depend on which model an agent calls.

## Model choice becomes an admin setting

The Anthropic deal fits the same design. Anthropic [committed to buy $30 billion of Azure compute](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/11/18/microsoft-nvidia-and-anthropic-announce-strategic-partnerships/) and contract up to 1 gigawatt more, Microsoft committed to invest up to $5 billion in Anthropic and Nvidia up to $10 billion, and Claude came to Microsoft Foundry, which Microsoft said makes Claude the only frontier model on all three major clouds. On November 20, GitHub put [enterprise bring-your-own-key](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-11-20-enterprise-bring-your-own-key-byok-for-github-copilot-is-now-in-public-preview) into public preview, so Copilot Business and Enterprise admins can connect Anthropic, Microsoft Foundry, OpenAI, and xAI keys, with usage billed by the provider and not counted against Copilot's quotas. That billing detail matters because Microsoft is letting the model bill go straight to another company as long as the seat and the admin console stay on GitHub. Nadella said something close to this on [the Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/satya-nadella-2) on November 12. He explained that he paused some data center leasing because he didn't want Azure to be a host for one model company on contracts with a short time horizon, and he said the code rival companies' agents write still ends up on GitHub. I wrote on November 9 that the release cadence [turns every new model into a decision about whether to re-test and switch](/writing/gpt-5-1-and-the-model-release-treadmill), and a company making that call every few weeks wants one place to switch models while the permissions stay put. What I take from this is that enterprises want model choice, and Microsoft wants to be the place where that choice is set and governed.

## The assistant is where Microsoft is weakest

CNBC's November 23 story also had a Microsoft partner saying some clients had gotten 50 percent off the $30 per user per month list price, though it added that Microsoft is pulling back on discounts. Microsoft says more than 90 percent of the Fortune 500 use Microsoft 365 Copilot, but that's a count of companies, with no seat numbers disclosed. CNBC did report a rollout at Land O'Lakes, which gave it to nearly 5,000 knowledge workers. At Ignite, Microsoft announced [Microsoft 365 Copilot Business](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft365copilotblog/introducing-microsoft-365-copilot-business-empowering-small-and-medium-businesse/4469700) at $21 per user per month for organizations with fewer than 300 users starting December 1, and said the Copilot Chat included with Microsoft 365 will get Agent Mode in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint in preview in early 2026. I think part of that is reaching smaller companies and people who don't pay for Copilot while the enterprise price holds at $30, but the moves also look like those of a company that finds the per-seat assistant a hard sell on its own. I'd call the assistant the place Microsoft is most exposed, which is why the governed layer matters more, since the registry and the permissions get used whichever lab's model a team prefers.

## The middleman problem

I think the most serious objection is the squeeze that hits middlemen, the companies that lose ground when their suppliers own the customer. On the Dwarkesh Podcast, co-host Dylan Patel, who founded SemiAnalysis, cited its estimates that Microsoft's share of the AI coding market fell from near 100 percent, or at least well above half, to under 25 percent in a year as Claude Code, Cursor, and OpenAI's Codex grew. Nadella answered that Microsoft has no birthright and that the category is probably going to be far bigger than anything it held high share in. Anthropic also keeps AWS as its primary cloud, which limits how much the Azure commitment ties it to Microsoft. On November 18, the day Ignite opened, the European Commission [launched Digital Markets Act investigations](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/commission-launches-market-investigations-cloud-computing-services-under-digital-markets-act-2025-11-18_en) into whether Azure and AWS should be designated gatekeepers, along with a third on cloud practices like tying and bundling. A control plane also needs agents in production to govern, and I'd guess most companies have only a few. The coding numbers worry me most, because if the estimate is close, a developer relationship can move very fast once the labs and startups sell direct. But the alternative is winning the assistant outright, and the CNBC reporting suggests that's the harder fight.

Microsoft's November decision was about where companies will decide which agents they trust. A company that can't win the product people ask for by name can still own the place where buyers set the rules for every product, and I think being that place is a stronger position than winning the assistant. Which assistant people prefer can shift with each model release, while the identities and permissions a company builds up in one registry get harder to move with every agent it adds. The risk is the middleman squeeze the coding numbers point to, where the labs keep the relationship with the people doing the work and Microsoft becomes the administration layer they sell through. I'd watch whether companies actually register other vendors' agents in Agent 365, and whether the labs keep shipping into Microsoft's products on launch day, the way Claude Opus 4.5 reached GitHub Copilot in public preview on November 24, the same day Anthropic released it. If both hold, conceding the assistant brand will look like a fair price for the layer a company's agents run inside.

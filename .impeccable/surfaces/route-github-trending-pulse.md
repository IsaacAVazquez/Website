# GitHub Trending Pulse surface brief

Written on 2026-10-12 by the stage 2 G6 agent (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-11-project-sovereign-design.md`, archetype Board).

Scope. The `/github-trending-pulse` route, rendered by `github-trending-client.tsx` with its own stylesheet `github-trending-pulse.css`, over the trimmed client snapshot from `getGitHubTrendingClientSnapshot`. The bar and language-share maths live in `star-log.ts` and its test.

Visitor mode. Read and filter. The visitor wants to see which repositories gained the most stars this week, inside a language or a topic.

Genre. `git log --stat` and GitHub's trending page, with a package registry's sortable list as the third reference. Every repository is one ruled line with its rank, its name in Fragment Mono, a one-line description, the week's gain with its measurement status, and the gain drawn as a bar, then total stars, language, pushed date, and the link from 1024px.

## Decisions

The star log and the table are one list. The board had been a capped twelve-row star log above a separate table that repeated the same names and gains, so the bar moved into the table as its own column from 640px and under the name on a phone, scaled to the biggest gain in the whole filter so unfolding the list never rescales it. A row opens in place on its name button or a pointer click, and the open row carries segments, topics, forks, issues, license, and score, plus stars, language, pushed, and the link under 1024px where those columns are hidden. The fold at 25 rows and "Show all N repositories" stayed. A selected repository below the fold opens the whole table, and the table stays open once it has, so closing that row leaves it on the page. Only a new filter folds the table again.

The bar is proportional to the gain, the way `git log --stat` draws it, with a 2% floor so a small gain still shows. In a week with one very large gain most bars sit at that floor. I looked at a square root scale on 2026-10-12 and kept the linear one, since a square root bar would draw a gain of 6K at about a quarter of an 80K bar with nothing on the page saying the scale had changed, and the figure already prints beside every bar. `star-log.test.ts` holds the proportion.

The first screen is a title row (the h1 in Fragment Mono, one sentence, and one status line with the source, the update time, the activity window, and the count of tracked repositories measured over the full window, plus the degraded-source line when it applies), then the board's toolbar with the Language and Topic switch, the segment switch, and the sort switch, then the language strip and the list. The status line prints the count ("155 of 165 tracked repositories measured over the full 7 days") and not a percentage, since a percentage of repositories read as a share of the stars gained. Figures print to one decimal from a thousand up.

The toolbar is sticky from 768px wide in a window at least 600px tall, so a landscape phone or a zoomed laptop does not give half its screen to the wrapped bar. While focus is in the list the window carries 17rem of scroll padding at the top, set on `html:has(.gt-list:focus-within)`, because a row focused under the stuck bar is still inside the window and no browser scrolls it clear by itself. The 17rem (272px) is the bar at its tallest plus one 44px name button and its 5px focus ring, because Firefox moves a focused row only once the whole of it is inside the padded area, so padding sized to the bar alone left the top of a button under it. The bar's tallest is 218px, in the topic view at 768 and 800 wide, measured on 2026-10-12 with the stylesheet loaded onto a reduced copy of the page's markup and not on the live page. On a phone the segment switch scrolls sideways in one row, with 5px of padding and a matching negative margin so the scroll box does not clip the focus ring, and the pressed segment is scrolled into view when a link opens on one past the edge (`useKeepPressedInView`). A Tab stop cut by that row's edge is scrolled whole into the row's padding when it takes focus (`revealFocusedSegment`), since a browser reveals a focused control only when almost none of it shows. The handler moves the row's own scroll and never the window.

The list sets `overflow-anchor: none`. A sort moves the rows and keeps their nodes, so scroll anchoring followed the row at the top of the window to its new rank and took the window with it, from 1200 to 426 at 900x800 on 2026-10-12. The cost is that closing an open row that sits above the window no longer holds the rows under it in place, so they move up by the height of the closed detail, which is what already happens when the open row is on screen. I reasoned that cost from how scroll anchoring picks its anchor and did not run it in a browser.

The route paints its own paper with `--gt-*` properties on `.gt-root`, a near-white `#fbfbf9` with graphite ink in light and `#101317` with pale ink in dark, and redeclares `--c97-surface`, `--c97-field`, `--c97-panel`, `--c97-ink`, `--c97-ink-2`, `--c97-label`, `--c97-rule`, `--c97-action`, and `--c97-accent` from them. The focus colour is a blue. The bars are a diff-addition green, solid when measured, hatched when partial, and outlined for a new baseline, with a legend under the strip. The language strip uses the language colours from GitHub's open linguist data, with a 2px paper gap between segments and a 1px outline in `--gt-ink-2` on every segment and on the Other key, since JavaScript's yellow, Rust's, Go's, and the grey all sit under 3:1 on the light paper and the outline holds on any paper. A segment is at least 5px wide, which leaves the smallest share its gap, its outline, and a pixel of colour. The caption names the lead language in words, counted over the base the share actually uses ("JavaScript has 30% of this week's star gain, counted across the N repositories in this view that gained stars"), since `languageShares` skips a flat or negative week. Every language outside the seven-colour map paints `--gt-other` grey, and the legend adds an "Other languages" key whenever the strip holds one. No typeface was added.

On paper the toolbar prints its pressed switches as ink text with no fill, the bars, the strip, and the legend keys keep their fills through `print-color-adjust: exact`, the fold button prints as the line that says the list runs past 25 rows, and an open row drops its stars, language, pushed, and link, which the printed columns already carry.

Cut, and not to be restored. The poster hero sheet and its torn seam, the three readouts (repos tracked, seven-day star delta, leading language), the "Snapshot leaders" rail, which repeated the segment switch, "The board" poster heading, the separate `StarLogBoard` component, the Link column's twin in the open row on wide screens, and the EmptyPanel card, which is a plain line now. The route stopped reading its `PROJECT_PRESS` row.

## Pinned, and kept

`src/app/github-trending-pulse/__tests__/github-trending-client.test.tsx` holds the h1, the region "GitHub trending filters" with the "Topic" and "TypeScript" buttons, the group "Sort repositories" with "Stars", the push URLs, the region "Repository table (scrolls sideways)" with 26 rows folded and 31 unfolded, "Show all 30 repositories", and the open row's Stars, Language, Pushed, and Repo. Two assertions changed on 2026-10-12. The filters-before test reads the table region, since the star-log list it read is merged into the table, and the language-share test reads the strip's caption, since the "Leading language" readout is cut. `e2e/product-surfaces.spec.ts` holds unchanged (`^Topic$`, `^Stars$`, `view=topic`, `sort=stars`, no overflow). The `view`, `segment`, `sort`, and `repo` parameters are unchanged. The same file now also holds the status line's count ("1 of 2 tracked repositories measured over the full 7 days"), the degraded line in the singular ("1 segment is using earlier data."), the table staying open at 31 rows after `repo=127` is cleared, and the strip's label reading a share that rounds to nothing as "under 1%". `__tests__/github-trending-css.test.ts` holds the strip outline and its contrast in both themes, the height condition and the `--c97-z-sticky` token on the sticky bar, the scroll padding rule, and the scroller's ring room. The client test also holds the segment row scrolling 60px for a focused button 55px past its edge and staying put for one already inside the padding, and the stylesheet test holds the list's `overflow-anchor: none`.

## Verified state, 2026-10-12

Against a production build (`next build --webpack`, served on 3800) in Chromium, `scripts/contrastSweep.mjs` found no failures in either theme on the default view, an open row, and a topic segment, and `scripts/layoutSweep.mjs` reported 0 fail, 0 review, and 0 report. The first row sits at y588 at 1440x900 and y802 at 390x844, against y1045 and y1030 for the first star-log row before (and y2376 and y2068 for the first table row). The keyboard walk from the page top reaches the kind switch at the 12th stop, right after the eleven header stops, Enter on Topic writes `view=topic`, and Space on the first name opens its row with `aria-expanded="true"`. Under reduced motion the switch transition resolves to 1e-05s. A Chromium print prints the title, the active switches only, the strip, and the list with the bar under each name across four Letter sheets. I did not check Safari's and Firefox's engines, a screen reader, or touch devices.

The later changes of 2026-10-12 (the strip outline, the height condition and scroll padding on the sticky bar, the scroller's ring room, the pressed segment scrolling into view, and the print rules) were reasoned from the cascade and covered by the two test files above. None of them was run in a browser, so the sweep, keyboard, and print results in the paragraph above describe the page before them.

The sort hold was measured on the production build on 2026-10-12 by adding the rule to the page in Chromium, Firefox's engine, and Safari's engine, where the window stayed at 1200 in all three, before the rule went into the stylesheet. The focus reveal was measured there with `scrollIntoView`, and the handler that shipped moves the row's scroll directly and was covered by the client test only.

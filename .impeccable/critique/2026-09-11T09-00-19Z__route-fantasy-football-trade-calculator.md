# Critique: Fantasy Football Trade Calculator (/fantasy-football/trade-calculator)

Scope note: this is the first critique of the trade calculator in its in-season state. The 2026 season opened on Wednesday 2026-09-09, `getNflRegularSeasonWeek` returns 1, and the surface renders the `SeasonalScopeNote` for the first time. The 2026-08-23 group loop scored it 29/40 in preseason; that run's six fixes (h1 wording, table rules, idle chip tone, clear disarm, combobox announcements, untruncated limits) all held when I checked them, and the two refuted findings from that run are not raised here. The populated state I drove was Ja'Marr Chase (WR, give) for Jahmyr Gibbs (RB, get), which is the pair the prompt asked for, then Houston DST added to the get side, then Chase for Christian Watson to see a wide gap.

## Design Specificity Verdict

The deal desk is still an authored composition. The settings rail, the two fieldsets with real legends, the pinned evaluation rail with an SVG balance, mono readouts, hairline plates, a single Instrument Serif italic limits line inside the estimate disclosure, and verdict sentences written in the first person could not be lifted from a fantasy site. Signal orange is spent on the selected scoring plate, the focused combobox, the scale marker, and focus rings, which is data, state, and action, and the heading tones for verdicts come from the status tokens rather than the accent. Where the desk goes generic is the package-fit table, which is a four-column stat grid that reads as furniture when both packages are one player, and the evidence-mix rows, which are a label-value list that any dashboard would produce.

Mechanical evidence from the round 1 sweep across all 16 cells (default and populated, light and dark, 390 through 1440): gate reproduced at 16.29 light and 15.28 dark, zero AA contrast failures out of 95 to 112 measured text nodes per cell with the floor at 4.80 (the note's muted copy on its warning-tinted wash), zero horizontal overflow, one `main` and one visible `h1` everywhere, zero unnamed sections, no heading skips, five live regions, 36 tab stops at 1440 with a visible ring on every one except the dev-tools portal, zero console errors, zero failed requests. In my own runs the two `?` tooltip triggers measure 44 by 44, every rail control and both remove buttons are 44px or taller, listbox options are 44px, the combobox is keyboard operable (ArrowDown, Enter, Escape, list removed from the DOM on close), the armed "Confirm clear" measures 150 by 44 and returns to "Clear" after five seconds without clearing, the URL after a settings change carries give, get, scoring, teams, rosterSize and lineup, and a reload restores both sides from `fantasy-trade-calculator-v1-2026-ppr`. Exactly one Instrument Serif element renders (the limits line, `trade-result-rail.tsx:292-301`).

Two mechanical flags I set aside as false positives. The three 16 by 16 scoring radios flag at 390 and 768 but not at 1024 and up. The difference is not the control. At 390 the radio rect sits at y 1002 with scrollY 0 after the sweep's `scrollIntoView`, because `html { scroll-behavior: smooth }` (`globals.css:38`) makes that scroll asynchronous, so the hit test ran on an off-screen point and returned null. At 1024 and up the sticky settings rail puts the radios inside the first viewport and no scroll is needed. The labels are 324 by 44 at 390 and 206 by 44 at 1440, which is the recorded decision. The second is a red `rgb(143,73,66)` reading on "fresh sources" in one run, which is the global 160ms colour transition caught midway between the pre-snapshot negative tone and ink-muted, and is the refuted load-flash finding, not a new one.

## Overall Impression

The desk itself is in good shape, and the honesty machinery that made it worth building is all still present and untruncated. What has changed since August is the calendar, and the calendar has exposed a seam. The page now opens with a bold, first-person promise that it will say nothing rather than price a frozen market, and then it prices the market anyway, with a green coverage chip and a fresh stamp, because the gate the note describes lives only in the note and the doc and not in the engine. On a surface whose entire pitch is that it withholds what it cannot support, that is the one thing that cannot be wrong. Fix the seam, route the in-season visitor to the weekly board like every sibling surface already does, and let the scale actually move, and this is a 33 or 34.

## What's Working

1. The two-step clear with a timed disarm is exactly right. Arm, wait, and it quietly returns to "Clear" without touching the deal (`trade-calculator-client.tsx:49-52, 247-251`), which is the August finding fixed properly rather than patched.
2. The combobox is a real combobox. `aria-expanded` tracks the popup, `aria-activedescendant` follows the arrow keys, the no-results message is a mounted live region, the duplicate shows as a disabled "Already added" option instead of vanishing, and Escape unmounts the list (`trade-player-combobox.tsx:238-290, 292-347`).
3. Limits are written as sentences and kept whole. "Why coverage is limited" opens to one plain line about unequal packages, the estimate explainer is two sentences, and the serif limits line names what the number is not. None of it is truncated, which is the August P1 that mattered most.

## Priority Issues

1. [P0] The page promises to withhold a verdict and then issues one. The note at `trade-calculator-client.tsx:393-398` reads "from here on it declines a verdict rather than reading a frozen board as a live one. I would rather it say nothing than say something I cannot support." Directly below it, Chase for Gibbs returns "Supported coverage / Balanced offer / The central values are within 5% of each other", Chase plus a DST returns "Limited coverage / Leans your way", and Chase for Watson returns "Clear edge to the other side". The header stamps both sources Sep 10, 2026 and prints "fresh sources". The reason is that the gate is date-driven and knows nothing about the week. `evaluateFantasyTrade` sets `marketUsable` from `getFantasyAdpFreshness(marketAsOf, season, now) === "current"` and a four-day staleness window (`fantasyTrade.ts:549-553`, `fantasyUtils.ts:588-595`), and the provider keeps stamping the board, so `public/data/fantasy/ppr.json` carries `adpSource.asOf` of 2026-09-10 with a sample of 3,631. `seasonWeek` is computed at `:340` and used only to show the note. The model doc says the opposite of the code too, "from Week 1 the coverage rule drops to insufficient and the tool declines verdicts for the rest of the season" (`docs/FANTASY_DRAFT_MODEL.md`, "What happens to the trade calculator in season"). Three artifacts, two positions. Why it matters: the reader has no way to know which sentence to trust, and the protected honesty framing is now the thing that is inaccurate. Fix: decide which is true and make the other two match. If the tool should go quiet in season, pass `seasonWeek` into the evaluation and make coverage insufficient from Week 1 with a warning that says so, and have the withheld copy name the season rather than "too old, incomplete, or unsupported". If the tool should keep pricing while the provider keeps publishing, rewrite the note to say what actually happens ("the mock-draft feed is still updating as of Sep 10, so the estimate still runs, but it prices a draft market and not in-season value; when the feed stops the verdict will be withheld"), retitle the tooltip at `trade-result-rail.tsx:211` away from "Current", and correct the doc paragraph. Suggested pass: harden.

2. [P1] An in-season visitor is not routed to anything live. The note is the only `SeasonalScopeNote` in the fantasy group with no link out. The rankings board (`fantasy-football-client.tsx:1621-1632`), redraft tracker (`draft-tracker-client.tsx:952-954`), mock draft (`mock-draft-client.tsx:744-749`) and best ball tracker (`best-ball/draft-tracker/draft-tracker-client.tsx:171-173`) each close their note with "Ranks that still move are on the weekly board" and a link. This one ends on "I cannot support" and the header's only action, "View rankings" (`trade-calculator-client.tsx:374-379`), goes to the draft board. Why it matters: the loop's standing question for every surface is whether a Week 1 visitor gets sent to what is live now, and here they are invited to price a September trade off August data with no pointer to `/fantasy-football/weekly` or `/fantasy-football/waivers`. Fix: end the note with the same sentence and link the siblings use, and consider making the header action "Weekly board" from Week 1. Suggested pass: clarify.

3. [P1] The memorable moment does not happen. The brief names the balance scale moving as a player is added as the memorable moment. Measured at 1440, the marker sits at `translateX(0px)` for Chase versus Gibbs (a 3.9% gap, because balanced returns `winner: null` and `markerPosition` returns centre, `trade-balance-scale.tsx:89-93`, `fantasyTrade.ts:507-513`), moves 11px of a 143px half-track for the 12% gap with a DST added, and only reaches 90px for Chase versus Watson, a 90% gap no one would offer. The track is linear in `relativeGap` up to 100%, so the entire 5% to 15% band the verdict vocabulary is built on occupies 7 to 21px. At 390 the rail's top lands at 856px in an 844px viewport after the second add, so the scale and the verdict are just below the fold while the combobox keeps focus; the live region announces the verdict but nothing on screen changes for Casey. Why it matters: the one animated element on the page reads as stuck for every realistic offer, and on the phone it is not visible at the moment it should move. Fix: scale the track to the verdict thresholds (centre band for balanced, the 15% clear-edge line at the tick marks, clamp beyond) so a lean is visibly off centre, keep the reduced-motion guard, and below `lg` either scroll the verdict into view on the first add or show a one-line sticky verdict strip under the site header once both sides have a player. Suggested pass: layout.

4. [P2] The idle rail contradicts the header. Before any player is added, "Draft market" prints "0/0" and "Not current" (`trade-result-rail.tsx:217-219`) 60px below a header that prints "Draft market Sep 10, 2026" and "fresh sources". "Not current" is the placeholder for "no result yet", but it is a staleness claim on the same source the header calls fresh. Fix: when `result` is null print "Waiting for players" or the source date, and reserve "Not current" for `market.usable === false`. Suggested pass: clarify.

5. [P2] A balanced offer wears the warning tone. `verdictCopy` maps `balanced` to `var(--home-warning)` (`trade-result-rail.tsx:29-34`), so "Balanced offer" renders amber in light (rgb 154,84,4) and yellow in dark (rgb 251,191,36), the same tone as "Verdict withheld". Balanced is the outcome the tool exists to find, not a caution. Fix: ink for balanced, keep warning for withheld. Suggested pass: quieter.

6. [P2] The withheld copy hedges instead of explaining. "The available inputs are too old, incomplete, or unsupported for a verdict" (`trade-result-rail.tsx:59-64`) names three causes and commits to none; the real cause is in the warnings behind a closed `details`. The prompt's question was whether a withheld verdict explains itself in plain words, and today it cannot be reached at all (DST, K, TE, and a rank-56 WR all produced verdicts), so the copy has never been exercised in production. Fix: pick the body from the first warning (stale board, missing market reading, unequal sides, or the season boundary from issue 1) so the heading and the reason sit together. Suggested pass: clarify.

7. [P2] Timestamps carry minute precision and wrap in the rail. `formatUpdatedAt` uses `timeStyle: "short"` (`fantasyUtils.ts:24-27`), so the rail prints "Sep 10, 2026, 12:19 AM" at 11px mono in a 137px cell that renders 31px tall with "AM" orphaned, while the Draft market row below fits on one 16px line, so the two rows misalign. A daily snapshot does not need the minute, and the market stamp is a date-only value the provider publishes as midnight. Fix: date-only in the rail (`dateStyle: "medium"`), keep the time in the header if it is wanted there. Suggested pass: polish.

## Persona Red Flags

Alex (impatient power user, desktop, mid-week trade talk): builds the deal in under twenty seconds, keyboard the whole way, and gets a sentence he can paste into a league chat. Then he reads the note, reads the green chip, and stops trusting either. He also cannot find the number that matters for a September trade anywhere on this page, and nothing tells him the weekly board exists.

Sam (screen reader and keyboard): the flow completes. The combobox announces the list and the no-results state, the count and the verdict are polite live regions, the two `?` triggers are 44px buttons that open on focus, and the balance is a labelled figure with an sr-only caption. Two snags. Tabbing out of an open combobox lands on the listbox `ul` itself, because `overflow-y: auto` (`trade-player-combobox.tsx:303`) makes the scroll container focusable in Chromium, so there is a stop with nothing to do before the remove button. And the verdict heading is a `<p>` (`trade-result-rail.tsx:173`) so the rail's only heading is "Evidence mix", which reads oddly in a headings list.

Casey (distracted, phone, one thumb): the phone stack is in the right order and every target is large. After she adds the second player the verdict is 12px below the bottom of the screen and the scale never moved where she could see it, so her first read is that nothing happened. The freshness row wraps to three lines with the separators dangling at the line ends, which is cosmetic but is the first thing under the h1.

## Minor Observations

- The h1 uses `clamp(2.25rem,1.7rem+2.5vw,4.2rem)` (`trade-calculator-client.tsx:361`), which is off the DESIGN.md headline ramp `clamp(2.15rem,1.6rem+2.75vw,4.2rem)`; it renders 63.2px at 1440 and 37px at 390, so the effect is small.
- 85.0 and 88.3 each appear twice on screen, once as the ledger row's "index" (16px mono) and once as the rail's readout (26px mono). The row copy could carry ADP and ECR and leave the value to the rail, or the rail could carry the values alone.
- The "index" unit under each row value (`trade-package-fieldset.tsx:105-107`) is never defined on the page; the estimate explainer calls it a "replacement-relative index" two disclosures away.
- At 1440 the centre ledger is about 300px tall with one player a side while both rails run 720 and 800px, so two thirds of the centre column is paper. The `xl:grid-cols-2` fieldsets could stack under `xl` to fill height, or the fit table could move up beside the ledger until packages are unequal.
- The package-fit table for a one-for-one is two identical rows of "1 → 1", "0 → 0", 0, 0. It only says something when packages are unequal; hiding it or collapsing it to a sentence until then would remove 250px of nothing.
- "Preseason redraft · Model v1" with a signal shield sits in the header 90px above "Week 1 of the 2026 season." Both are true (scope versus calendar) but they read as a second contradiction on a page that already has one.
- The tooltip on Draft market says "Current mock-draft average position" (`trade-result-rail.tsx:211`); "Current" is the word the note says no longer applies.
- The freshness stamp row at 390 wraps its "·" separators onto line ends; the separators could be dropped below `sm`.
- `SeasonalScopeNote` uses `text-sm` but the note's copy measures 16px at 1440 and 14.3px at 390 because `.home-dash` scales the base size; the muted copy still clears AA at 4.80.
- Dark mode is at parity everywhere I looked; the warning border on the note is 54% yellow on near-black and reads as intended.
- The shared Contact CTA h2 renders at 44px against the 63.2px h1 at 1440, correct order here; inherited shell, not a fantasy finding.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the provider keeps publishing mock-draft ADP through the season, which it did on Sep 10 with a 3,631-draft sample, is the honest position that the market is "frozen", or that it is a live market for the wrong question? The note and the doc both argue frozen; the data says otherwise.
2. The scale is the surface's one expressive element and it moves 11px for a lean. Would the verdict thresholds (5% and 15%) make a better axis than the raw gap, so the marker crosses a visible line exactly when the words change?
3. Every sibling surface hands the Week 1 visitor to the weekly board. Should this surface go further and hide the ledger behind the note from Week 1, or is a running calculator with a clear boundary more useful than a closed one?
4. The withheld verdict has never rendered in production. Is there a way to exercise it in the sweep, for instance a `?asOf=` override or a fixture snapshot, so the honesty copy is checked before the day it is needed?
